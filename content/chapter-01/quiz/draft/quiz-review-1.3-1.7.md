## Full drafts · 1.3 Hazard warning symbols & lab safety

### 13-1
- **stem:** Which hazard warning symbol applies to ethanol used in the laboratory?
- **options:** A. Flammable · B. Explosive · C. Corrosive · D. Irritant
- **answer:** A · **hint:** Ethanol is kept away from heat and flame.

### 13-2
- **stem:** Which precaution is most appropriate when handling a corrosive chemical such as hydrochloric acid?
- **options:**
  - A. Keep away from heat and flame only
  - B. Avoid shock that may cause an explosion
  - C. Wear safety goggles and protective gloves
  - D. Open all windows and turn off the gas supply
- **answer:** C · **hint:** Corrosive substances can damage skin and eyes.

### 13-3
- **stem:** Which hazard warning symbol would you expect on dynamite?
- **options:** A. Flammable · B. Explosive · C. Toxic · D. Corrosive
- **answer:** B · **hint:** Explosives must be kept away from heat, flame, and shock.

### 13-4
- **stem:** What is the main function of a fire blanket?
- **options:**
  - A. Flush chemicals from the eyes
  - B. Protect clothing from spills
  - C. Provide ventilation for toxic fumes
  - D. Smother a small fire by cutting off the oxygen supply
- **answer:** D · **hint:** Removing oxygen is one way to break the fire triangle.

### 13-5
- **stem:** If chemicals get into the eyes, what should you do first?
- **options:**
  - A. Wash the eyes with distilled water from an eye wash bottle
  - B. Rub the eyes firmly to remove the chemical
  - C. Place the eyes under a sand bucket
  - D. Turn off the gas supply and open the windows
- **answer:** A · **hint:** Use the eye wash bottle to flush and cleanse the eyes.

## Full drafts · 1.5 Measurement

### 15-1
- **stem:** What is the numerical meaning of the prefix kilo- (k)?
- **options:** A. 0.001 · B. 1000 · C. 0.01 · D. 100
- **answer:** B · **hint:** kilo- means thousand (e.g. 1 km = 1000 m).

### 15-2
- **stem:** When measuring liquid volume with a measuring cylinder, where should you read the scale?
- **options:**
  - A. At the top of the meniscus
  - B. At the average of the top and bottom of the meniscus
  - C. At the level of the bottom of the meniscus
  - D. From the side at an angle below the liquid surface
- **answer:** C · **hint:** Read at the bottom of the meniscus, looking from vertically above where appropriate.

### 15-3
- **stem:** Which statement about volume units is correct?
- **options:**
  - A. 1 mL = 1 cm³
  - B. 1 mL = 1000 cm³
  - C. 1 L = 1 cm³
  - D. 1 m³ = 1000 cm³
- **answer:** A · **hint:** Millilitre and cubic centimetre are equal in volume.

### 15-4
- **stem:** When finding the volume of foam cubes by displacement in a measuring cylinder, why is a metal block needed?
- **options:**
  - A. The metal block measures temperature
  - B. Foam would float on water
  - C. Foam dissolves in water
  - D. The metal block removes the meniscus
- **answer:** B · **hint:** Foam floats, so a denser object is used to help submerge it for displacement.

### 15-5
- **stem:** Convert 6 L to millilitres (mL).
- **options:** A. 6 mL · B. 60 mL · C. 600 mL · D. 6000 mL
- **answer:** D · **hint:** 1 L = 1000 mL, so 6 × 1000 = 6000 mL.

## Full drafts · 1.6 Bunsen burner *(note MCQ + key)*

### 16-1
- **stem:** What is the first step when using a Bunsen burner?
- **options:**
  - A. Turn on the gas tap
  - B. Place the Bunsen burner on a heat-proof mat
  - C. Open the air hole fully
  - D. Light the flame
- **answer:** B

### 16-2
- **stem:** What happens if you open the air hole fully?
- **options:**
  - A. The flame becomes yellow and quiet
  - B. The flame becomes blue and noisy
  - C. The flame burns inside the chimney, causing strike back
  - D. The flame extinguishes immediately
- **answer:** C

### 16-3
- **stem:** What is the purpose of closing the air hole before lighting the Bunsen burner?
- **options:**
  - A. To make the flame blue
  - B. To ensure a luminous flame for easier ignition
  - C. To increase the temperature of the flame
  - D. To reduce noise levels
- **answer:** B

### 16-4
- **stem:** Which of the following conditions indicates a non-luminous flame?
- **options:**
  - A. Yellow colour, irregular shape, quiet
  - B. Blue colour, regular shape, noisy
  - C. Green colour, hissing sound
  - D. Yellow colour, regular shape, quiet
- **answer:** B

### 16-5
- **stem:** What should you do if you observe a green flame and hear hissing sounds?
- **options:**
  - A. Adjust the collar to close the air hole
  - B. Turn off the gas tap immediately
  - C. Touch the chimney to fix the problem
  - D. Fully open the air hole
- **answer:** B · **hint:** That is strike back — do not touch the chimney or collar.

## Full drafts · 1.7 Invention / discovery / branches / other investigations

### 17-1
- **stem:** Gravity is an example of a …
- **options:** A. Invention · B. Discovery · C. Model · D. Classification
- **answer:** B · **hint:** Gravity already existed in nature; it was uncovered, not created.

### 17-2
- **stem:** An airplane is an example of a …
- **options:** A. Invention · B. Discovery · C. Pattern · D. Branch of geology
- **answer:** A · **hint:** An invention is a new device or method that did not previously exist.

### 17-3
- **stem:** Experimenting with acids and alkalis to understand pH levels belongs mainly to which branch of science?
- **options:** A. Physics · B. Biology · C. Chemistry · D. Astronomy
- **answer:** C

### 17-4
- **stem:** Investigating the formation and evolution of stars and galaxies belongs mainly to which branch of science?
- **options:** A. Geology · B. Medicine · C. Chemistry · D. Astronomy
- **answer:** D

### 17-5
- **stem:** Comparing similarities and differences between objects and sorting those with similarities into groups is called …
- **options:** A. Classifying · B. Pattern seeking · C. Model building · D. Discovery
- **answer:** A · **hint:** Pattern seeking looks for regularities; model building represents things too big or small to see.
